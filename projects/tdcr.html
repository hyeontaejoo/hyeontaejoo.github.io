MECHANICAL SYSTEM

Mechanical Design

Mechanical architecture for independent bending and extension/retraction while maintaining structural support and integrating sensing, actuation, and end-effector hardware.

The TDCR consists of two independently actuated continuum segments designed to provide both bending and extension/retraction . Combining the two motion modes expands the reachable workspace and increases the flexibility of the robotic system.

MODELING

PCC Kinematic Model

A Piecewise Constant Curvature kinematic model is used to represent the continuum robot configuration and predict robot shape and tip position.

  • Continuum shape representation
  • Forward tip-position prediction
  • Workspace visualization
INVERSE KINEMATICS

Damped Least-Squares IK

Damped Least-Squares inverse kinematics calculates the robot configuration required to move the end-effector toward a desired target position.

  • Target-to-shape calculation
  • Numerically stable IK
  • Trajectory planning
FEEDBACK CONTROL

IMU-Based Feedback

IMU orientation measurements are used to compare the desired and actual segment posture, enabling closed-loop posture correction.

  • Segment orientation sensing
  • Orientation error calculation
  • Closed-loop correction
MOTION CONSTRAINT

Bending-Plane Constraint

Motion outside the desired bending plane is suppressed to reduce unnecessary movement and improve trajectory consistency.

  • Reduced unnecessary motion
  • Improved path consistency
  • Controlled bending direction

PROJECT OUTCOME

From mechanical concept to an experimentally validated continuum robotic platform.

The project demonstrates a two-segment continuum robotic platform combining mechanical design, PCC-based kinematic modeling, DLS inverse kinematics, IMU-based closed-loop control, trajectory-planned physical motion, and an integrated three-finger gripper mechanism.
